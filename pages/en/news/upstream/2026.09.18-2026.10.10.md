This article outlines LoongArch upstream activities in the past few weeks (up to UTC+8 18:00, October 10th, 2026), including ports, optimization, platform support, bug fixes and reports, etc. in Linux kernel, compiler toolchains, emulators, and other projects.

> Editor's note: Our weekly updates kept growing in volume in the past months and the editing and translation has overwhelmed our editorial team. Starting this week, we will experiment with an "illustrated periodical" format, hoping to provide you with a better reading experience (and that these get published in a more timely manner).

Highlights in this issue:

- IPFire added LoongArch support, maintainer praising LoongArch's high level of upstream support
- FreeBSD's upstreaming LoongArch support under construction
- Firefox enhanced LoongArch SIMD optimization capabilities and introduced profiling support
- QEMU received LA664 core emulation support
- UEFI firmware 202608 community preview released

## IPFire Added LoongArch Support

This week, IPFire maintainer Michael Tremer [announced](https://www.ipfire.org/blog/yes-another-architecture-ipfire-now-builds-for-loongarch) LoongArch support on the project's blog - starting this week, IPFire will release nightly LoongArch test images along with other architectures.

Michael Tremer mentions in the post that the port is being built on a Loongson 3A6000 (XA61200_V1.1) build machine donated by the Loongson Hobbyists' Community donated, which offers good performance and usability and can complete a full build of IPFire in just two hours.

![3A6000 Motherboard donated by the Loongson Hobbyists' Community](/images/news/upstream/20261010-ipfire-3a6000.jpg)

The blog post also praises the high level of upstream support for LoongArch:

> [LoongArch] was the easiest port yet... [p]artly because LoongArch did its homework: support is upstream in the Linux kernel, GCC and the rest of the toolchain, and pretty much everywhere else it needs to be. This is not an architecture in its early days. It has arrived.

We would like to thank friends of the community for supplying the aforementioned hardware at lower-than-market prices. Your donations are supporting the construction of LoongArch's upstream ecosystem.

## FreeBSD LoongArch Port in Progress

Over the past month, FreeBSD's kernel, `pkg`, and other components saw LoongArch patches submitted or merged.

According to upstream contributor hitmoon:

> We still have a long way to go, with upstream review is still ongoing. The EFI loader has undergone a round of improvements based on upstream feedback. Substantive review of the kernel has not yet covered much, especially with regards to interrupts and memory, and we are still waiting for more comments from upstream reviewers. For our users, the port now build successfully and is usable on QEMU virtual machines, so you can try it out a bit.

@觉 from the Hangzhou Dianzi University is working on 3A6000 support, and he introduced:

> Writing drivers for real hardware has been a challenge, but it can now run and I haven't encountered any serious bugs yet. My current plan is mainly to get familiar with the kernel code and, while reading the code, I'm also helping with the review. Later, once I'm familiar enough, I plan to write a LoongArch wiki on wiki.freebsd.org (I have permission because I have previously participated in a related GSoC project). As for the driver issues, I will postpone working on them until the code is a bit more stable.

The Loongson Hobbyists' Community donated a Loongson 3A6000 desktop to support his porting and maintenance work.

We will continue to cover further progress in the \*BSD space - stay tuned!

## Firefox: Performance Optimizations and Profiling Support

Over the past month or so, Rong "Mantle" Bao [added](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=2075633) LoongArch SIMD extension (LSX)-based SIMD capabilities to the SpiderMonkey JavaScript engine. These capabilities apply to WebAssembly SIMD features, regex acceleration, and more. LoongArch has since become the 3rd architecture following x86/AMD64 (SSE4.1) and Arm/AArch64 (NEON) to support this feature.

![Firefox's SIMD capabilities (Source: Rong "Mantle" Bao)](/images/news/upstream/20261010-firefox-simd.png)

In addition, he [optimized](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=2077020) instruction selection for comparison operations in the above implementation when the right-hand side is a constant, and submitted [multi-byte string operation acceleration](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=2075954), [HTML parsing acceleration (htmlaccel)](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=2075954), and [DOM CharacterDataBuffer SIMD acceleration](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=2078673) powered by the LSX extension. He also fixed [fixed](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=2077820) the use of `B`/`BL` instructions with a smaller jump range in some assembly code.

Taken together, the above work resulted in a ~10% performance uplift in Firefox according to results from the Speedometer 3.1 browser benchmark.

> Note: While maintaining the Firefox package for AOSC OS, Rong "Mantle" Bao found that by enabling Profile-Guided Optimization, it is possible to achieve a further 20% performance improvements in Speedometer 3.1.

![Firefox Profiler Demo (Source: Rong "Mantle" Bao)](/images/news/upstream/20261010-firefox-profiler.png)

Further yet, several months ago, he also submitted LoongArch performance profiler support to Firefox. After several months of (frankly not very active) upstream review, the feature finally landed in the 160 release. This feature enables developers to analyze performance bottlenecks and potential implementation issues, helping them implement more architecture-specific optimizations.

## QEMU: LA664 Emulation Support

In late September, Bibo Mao [submitted](https://www.mail-archive.com/qemu-devel%40nongnu.org/msg1225799.html) a patchset that adds LA664 processor core emulation to QEMU, implementing LoongArch ISA 1.1 features, KVM host capability detection, hardware page table walk (HPTW), and DINTC support, ending the era where QEMU could only emulate the LA464 core.

Of course, emulation support for other processor cores such as LA264, LA364, and LA364E remains to be implemented. T developers can contact the Loongson Hobbyists' Community, and the staff can help coordinate work and necessary information such as technical materials.

## UEFI Firmware Update: 202608 Community Preview

At the end of September, maintainers including Dongyan Qian (MarsDoge) and Zixuan Lu released the 202608 community preview of the UEFI firmware, covering current desktop and server products in the 2K3000/3B6000M and 6000 series and including important updates.

It is worth mentioning that this firmware release also includes the update for the Loongson 3A6000 NUC (model code QA612N0), users using such small form factor machines can finally update their firmware!

![Loongson 3A6000 NUC running the 202608 firmware: a long-waited update!](/images/news/upstream/20261010-202608-uefi-on-nuc.jpg)

Here are a few important changes:

- Fixed the XA612A0 board's I²C voltage regulation channel setting the core voltage too high; users running versions 202602/05 should update to this version as soon as possible
- Enabled HPTW (Hardware Page Table Walk) capabilities on 3B6000 and 3C6000 series platforms, recommended for use with Loongnix Server 23.3 (Linux 6.6.52-1.12) or mainline 7.1.5 and above kernel versions
- Fixed abnormal maximum frequency display after enabling DVFS on the 3B6000 platform
- Improved ESP boot entry naming to identify operating systems such as Loongnix, UOS, Kylin, openEuler, deepin, Anolis, Arch Linux, and AOSC OS from known system boot paths

The changelog also mentions that this firmware version "optimized some atomic operations". Testing shows that this firmware version works around the LA664 atomic operation lost update issue introduced by Jiajie Chen at the end of September, so users of processor platforms based on the LA664 core (the entire 6000 series family) are strongly advised to update. For an introduction to this issue, we recommend reading Jiajie Chen's blog post ["One CPU Atomic Instruction, One Packaging Infinite Loop: The Story of the Lost Update on LA664"](https://jia.je/hardware/2026/09/24/loongson-cpu-erratum-en/).

> Editor's note: If your LA664 device does not have firmware available in the loongson/Firmware repository (such as third-party hardware solutions or those using commercial firmware from Kunlun, Byosoft, etc.), please refer to the above information and contact the manufacturer or distributor to obtain a firmware update.

Finally, during testing we found that some "early" batches of AC612A0_V1.0 (single-socket 3C6000/S) motherboards require switching the power chip mode to I²C in the firmware settings and disabling the kernel `ls2x-i2c` driver. This may be a compatibility issue caused by changes to the power control chip specifications without a corresponding change to the motherboard model. Maintainers are exploring ways to resolve or work around this issue.

For other changes, see the [changelog](https://github.com/loongson/Firmware/blob/main/docs/changelist_V5.0.0639-stable202608-CN.md). You can download firmware from the [loongson/Firmware GitHub repository](https://github.com/loongson/Firmware). The Loongson Hobbyists' Community staff are compiling the materials and expect to publish the firmware update and newly added hardware model information on the community portal this week.

## Linux Kernel: 2K0300 Mainlining, Hardware Page Table Walk, and Soft Lockup Fixes

Over the past month, work in the Linux kernel mainly consisted of patchset revisions and small cleanups, including the [Loongson Security Engine multi-node support fix (9th revision)](https://lore.kernel.org/loongarch/20261008073151.15808-1-zhaoqunqin@163.com/) submitted by Qunqin Zhao, and patchsets including [2K0300 temperature sensor support (7th revision)](https://lore.kernel.org/loongarch/cover.1791512067.git.zhoubinbin@loongson.cn/) and [DMA controller support (2nd revision)](https://lore.kernel.org/loongarch/cover.1791535540.git.zhoubinbin@loongson.cn/) submitted by Binbin Zhou; Chaithanya Lagisetty [submitted](https://lore.kernel.org/loongarch/20260929052712.470413-1-nagachaithanya9911@gmail.com/) a patch that fixes the return value of `kvm_pch_pic_create()` always being `-ENOMEM` (Bibo Mao subsequently pointed out that [Tao Cui had already submitted a similar fix](https://lore.kernel.org/lkml/20260927075240.3007947-6-cui.tao@linux.dev/)).

In terms of new features, Bibo Mao [implemented](https://lore.kernel.org/loongarch/20260923012356.1058379-1-maobibo@loongson.cn/) automatic detection support for the KVM hardware page table walker (HPTW), which can be automatically enabled on platforms that support this feature (currently the 3B6000 and 3C6000 series), bringing potential performance improvements.

In addition, at the end of September, Tiezhu Yang [submitted](https://lore.kernel.org/all/20260908073906.31781-1-yangtiezhu@loongson.cn/) a fix for a CPU soft lockup triggered by the `timer_lockup` test. Tiezhu Yang noted that LoongArch is a weak memory ordering architecture, and the combination of relaxed `atomic_inc()` + `smp_mb__after_atomic()` in the current implementation is insufficient to guarantee the ordering of store and load operations. The latter is defined as compiler barrier (e.g., an empty inline assembly statement), which contradicts the `smp_mb__after_atomic()` function documentation that states "it can be compiled to a no-op on TSO architectures". Other weak memory ordering architectures also do not define it as a no-op, but instead directly use a full memory barrier (equivalent to LoongArch's `dbar 0`).

> Editor's note: TSO (Total Store Order) is a memory consistency model in computer architecture.

The fix has now been merged into the loongarch-next branch and is expected to enter the mainline kernel soon. Given that this behavioral fix affects many hardware drivers and critical kernel functions and behaviors, distributions may consider picking it up early and arranging tests.

## A report of a perf compilation failure

Recently, while building ULoong, an unofficial LoongArch distribution for Ubuntu, community developers found that the perf tool configured for Ubuntu failed to build (with a link-time error). After community developers reported the issue, Meng Qinggang [submitted](https://inbox.sourceware.org/binutils/20260919085650.2568760-1-mengqinggang@loongson.cn/) a patch that makes `ld -r` insert symbols instead of relocating against section + offset. After upstream maintainer Alan Modra strongly opposed that patch, Meng Qinggang [submitted](https://inbox.sourceware.org/binutils/20260923095431.2702807-1-mengqinggang@loongson.cn/) another patch that enables LoongArch's linker relaxation logic to handle relocations against section + offset.

Testing showed that the new patch failed to resolve the original perf tool link failure in the ULoong port, and the issue awaits further investigation.
